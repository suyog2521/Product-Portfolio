Pilgrim is a D2C beauty and personal-care brand (haircare, skincare, makeup) on Shopify. When I joined, the product team had no product-driven analytics — decisions ran off Shopify's built-in funnel, directional totals with no user-path or click-level detail. If we changed a specific section on a product page, there was no way to measure whether it moved behavior. That single gap blocked hypothesis-forming and made experiments unmeasurable at the level that mattered. A prior attempt to fix this dumped every event into BigQuery with no structure — it cost ₹3 lakh in one month and was shut down.

Technical PM

Product Analytics Pipeline, Built Solo

The Mandate

Two targets, deliberately in tension: stand up real product analytics for web and app, and keep it cheap — the last attempt had died on cost. The analytics team didn't have bandwidth to take it on, so rather than let it stall, I took the entire build end-to-end myself — design, instrumentation, data modeling, and cost control — and shipped it without dedicated engineering support.

What I Built
  • Collection — storefront + checkout events from Shopify, plus a custom analytics provider inside the React Native app, sent to a single collector.
  • Flow — collector (Cloud Run) → object storage → a loader on a 15-minute schedule → BigQuery, landing in a clean, analysis-ready table (items flattened, funnel stages, attribution, click detail).
  • Coverage — 13 events across the full funnel (page view → product view → add-to-cart → checkout steps → purchase), for both web and app.
  • The key product unlock — section-level click tracking. Every click inside a tagged page section is captured with its section name. For the first time, "we changed this section — did behavior change?" became a question we could actually answer.

Web pipeline went live May 7; the app pipeline June 22.

Holding Cost at ~₹14K (the discipline)

The previous attempt blew up because it captured everything, indiscriminately. I captured deliberately:

  • Filtered click capture to only tagged sections, not every click on the page — the single biggest cost lever.
  • Raw event data auto-expires after 30 days; raw files auto-delete after 3.
  • Capped compute instances and error-only logging.
Analytics pipeline cost₹3L → ₹14K/month
Cost reduction~95%, verified against actual billing
What the Pipeline Unlocked

Checkout diagnosis during End of Season Sale. Checkout conversion dropped sharply during our biggest sale. Because I'd instrumented checkout validation errors, I could see exactly which errors were firing and rank the dominant drop reason — turning "checkout is down" into a specific, fixable cause and a product decision during the sale window, not after it.

  • 97% of paid users make a binary buy-or-leave decision on the landing product page and never explore further. The ~3% who do explore first convert roughly 10x higher — invisible in a Shopify funnel, now a clear roadmap bet around nudging exploration.
  • A coupon-awareness gap at checkout — ~1,200 checkout sessions on a sale day entered with a cart that qualified for an active discount, never applied it, and abandoned. A pure awareness failure, fixable by surfacing the eligible offer at checkout entry, with six figures of daily revenue identified as recoverable.

I treated data trust as a feature rather than presenting the data as perfect — documenting exact capture rates (web ~86% of orders, app ~98%) and keeping a prioritized known-issues backlog, so every analyst knew precisely how far to trust each number.

What I'd do differently / next
  • Close the ~14% web order gap (certain UPI payment redirects skip tracking) with a server-side order webhook.
  • Build a daily summary layer for clean week-over-week and YoY trends.
  • Push the surfaced insights (exploration nudge, checkout coupon surfacing) into shipped A/B tests and measure the lift — moving from "insights enabled" to "revenue moved."

On a team with no measurement, the highest-leverage thing a PM can do is remove the dependency that blocks every other experiment — the ability to ask a product question and get a trustworthy answer. The pipeline was never the goal; being able to ask and answer product questions was.

Pluckk is a fresh-produce business — value-added FMCG, raw and cut fruit/veg, D+1 delivery. It began D2C, but thin margins and high CAC made pure ad-driven D2C unsustainable, so the company pivoted to B2B as the primary channel (supplying quick-commerce players like Blinkit, Instamart, Zepto, BigBasket), keeping B2C alive on organic demand only.

Two flows, two very different problems. B2B: customer sends an indent → we raise a PO to local vendors → warehouse cutting/packing → deliver against the customer's purchase order — reconciliation-heavy. B2C: we forecast SKU-level demand daily and stock to that forecast — perishability means any forecast error turns directly into waste. I owned one hero project on each side.

Ops · B2C

Dynamic Liquidation Engine + Sales-Projection Rewrite

Problem

The B2C sales-projection logic hadn't changed in a long time — a flat 7-day average across a few variables. It over-projected some SKUs and under-projected others. Over-projected perishables with 1–2 day shelf life went to dump; overall ~10–15% of inventory was being written off. Liquidation existed but was manual: the team eyeballed the top-5 highest-margin-loss SKUs and discounted only those — which often weren't the right SKUs to save.

Insight

Two problems were tangled together. One was a reaction problem (we clear expiring stock badly) and one was a root-cause problem (we stock the wrong quantities to begin with). They needed different solutions, in sequence.

Part A — Liquidation Engine (stop the bleeding)

A dynamic, predictive pricing engine replacing a static evening markdown. At 5 PM it takes a real-time sales snapshot and predicts whether today's remaining expiring stock will clear by midnight on its own momentum. Only if a genuine shortfall is projected does it trigger a tiered discount sized to the exact surplus — protecting margin instead of blanket-discounting.

The engine logic (5 steps)

Variables: ES = stock expiring today · ARR = today's actual hourly run-rate (12 AM–5 PM) · HRR_day = median historical hourly rate, same window, last 7 days · HRR_evening = median historical hourly rate, 5 PM–midnight · EES = expected evening sales.

  1. Normalize history — strip past liquidation noise from evening data using an uplift factor (discounted-day sales ÷ normal-day baseline), so HRR_evening reflects true organic demand, not past fire-sales.
  2. Measure today's momentum — Trend Ratio = ARR ÷ HRR_day (>1 = faster than usual, <1 = slower).
  3. Predict tonight — EES = HRR_evening × Trend Ratio × 7 remaining hours.
  4. Two decision gates (both must hold, to avoid panic-discounting): (a) projected leftover — ES > EES; (b) today genuinely lagging — (HRR_day − ARR) ÷ HRR_day > 0.2.
  5. Size the discount to the surplus — 10–20% surplus → 15% off · 21–50% → 20% off · >50% → 30% off.

Output: every day at 5 PM a "Deals of Day" section went live on app + web, ordered by demand, with a WhatsApp trigger to drive traffic to it.

Part B — Sales-Projection Rewrite (fix the root cause)

Rewrote the projection model to move from a flat historical average to a momentum- and seasonality-aware forecast. As accuracy improved, fewer SKUs over-stocked, so progressively less inventory ever entered the liquidation pool in the first place.

Impact
Ageing-inventory losses (liquidation engine)↓ 55%
Overstock-driven liquidation (projection rewrite)↓ ~30%
Deals of Day engagement~30% of DAU (~10–12k)
Deals of Day conversion vs. normal2.2x
Time to ship V11.5 months

The two reductions measure different things: the engine recovered value from stock already heading to dump; the projection rewrite shrank how much got over-ordered in the first place. Together they attacked waste from both ends.

Ops · B2B

Automated SO–PO Reconciliation

Problem

Every Sales Order (~200–300/day across India's warehouses) had to be reconciled against the customer's Purchase Order and booked in Zoho Books for finance. Finance did this by hand — 2–3 hrs/day matching quantity, price and GST, and chasing credit/debit notes whenever a shortfall or price change occurred. Slow booking also slowed payment collection.

Insight

This wasn't just a task to automate — it was a decision-routing problem. Clean matches should book themselves; only genuine exceptions deserve a human. Design goal: nothing books wrong, and nothing falls through silently.

Solution

A daily job that, for every unbooked SO: extracts the PO number → scrapes the relevant customer email from our inbox → uses OCR to pull PO and credit/debit-note data into our DB → matches against the SO on quantity, price and GST.

  • Match clean → auto-book in our system and Zoho.
  • Mismatch / missing note → hold and route to finance/sales for manual reconciliation, with the discrepancy surfaced.

I owned the direction end-to-end with finance and ops — what to extract, from where, what to match on, and the exception-routing rules. Engineering selected the OCR model and built the pipeline.

Impact
Sales orders auto-booked, no human touch70%
Manual reconciliation effort removed2–3 hrs/day
Remaining 30%auto-flagged with context
Downstreamfaster billing → improved receivables
Added benefitaccountability on last-minute price changes
What I'd do differently
  • Liquidation engine: A/B test the discount slabs themselves rather than setting them by judgment — the 15/20/30% tiers were reasoned, not empirically tuned.
  • Projection rewrite: build a forecast-vs-actual accuracy dashboard (by SKU) from day one, so the improvement was provable per-SKU.
  • Reconciliation: treat the 70% auto-book rate as a tracked health metric with a target to push higher over time.

Both projects came from the same instinct: on a thin-margin business, the highest-leverage product work is often operational, not user-facing. In both, the real design judgment lived in the guardrails — when not to discount, and when not to auto-book.

Growth

The Female Cohort Funnel

Problem

When I joined, ~30% of traffic was female, but female form-completion and purchase conversion were both meaningfully lower than male. It looked like a targeting problem. It wasn't.

Insight

The entire brand was male-coded at every touchpoint — male celebrity banners, a hair-test form with identical questions for both genders, and a result page built around male-pattern root causes. A female user landing in this funnel didn't see herself in it, so she left.

What We Built — a parallel female funnel across all four touchpoints
  • Ads (with growth team) — separate female campaigns with assurance-driven, emotionally resonant creative rather than feature-led messaging.
  • Landing page — female-specific LP leading with female root causes (hormonal imbalance, PCOD, PCOS) and reassurance-oriented copy.
  • Hair-test form — redesigned with health-context questions (hormonal history, pregnancy, menstrual health). Insight: female users wanted to explain more about their health — giving them that space lifted completion.
  • Result page — proactively answered the real objections (sourced from hair-coach call logs) — will this affect my periods? can pregnant women take it? — removing the final friction at the point of decision.
Impact
Female funnel conversion2.5% → 5.3%
Female daily revenue₹1.75L → ₹22.5L

Female revenue grew ~13x — faster than overall revenue — by making the funnel speak to this cohort specifically.

Retention

The Retention Login Module

Problem

Traya's treatment works over 3–5 months, but Month-2 repurchase sat at ~33%. Two of three Month-1 buyers didn't return. Hair coaches called on Day 20, but most calls went unanswered — users were at work, travelling, or wanted to decide on their own terms. The entire reorder decision sat with the user, with no product support.

Insight

The hair coach was the only bridge between Month 1 and Month 2. The product had no equivalent. We needed to build that bridge inside the product.

What We Built — a personalized login module
  • User profile built from Month-1 treatment data.
  • Countdown timer triggered on Day 20 ("7 days remaining for your next kit").
  • Pre-configured Month-2 recommendation — no guesswork on what to reorder.
  • Results timeline setting progress expectations to keep users motivated through a slow-burn treatment.
  • WhatsApp retention segments — non-repurchasers segmented (call-not-picked / interested-but-delayed) and sent personalized reorder URLs.

V1 shipped in 20 days from ideation. I defined the segmentation logic and customer-data architecture — what to pull from the treatment API, what to surface at which moment, and which behavioral triggers would cut decision friction.

Impact
Month-2 repurchase rate33% → 48%

+46% lift. The same infrastructure compounded across M3, M4, and M5, lifting LTV across the full treatment journey — not a one-time fix, a retention system.

Activation

App Onboarding + CMS

Problem

We began building the app in early 2023. Early app traffic was almost entirely organic — users who'd heard of Traya, downloaded, signed up, and landed on a generic homepage. Drop-off was high and uninstalls were climbing.

Insight

Organic app users ≠ ad-driven web users. Ad users arrived pre-educated by top-of-funnel creative. Organic users arrived with intent but no context — they'd done the work of downloading and signing up (a high-intent signal), then hit a homepage with no narrative, creating decision fatigue. High intent ≠ high activation.

What We Built — a segmented onboarding layer

A personalized onboarding-video layer managed through an internal CMS tool I spec'd with engineering:

  • New users (awareness stage 1–3) — brand-explainer videos: what Traya is, how it works.
  • New users (stage 4–5) — urgency-oriented messaging: why timing matters.
  • Web-to-app users (already filled the hair test) — skip basics, go straight to treatment context.
  • Repeat buyers — progress reinforcement.

The CMS was template-based by design — new segments could be created without engineering, keeping the content layer owned by product, not blocked on sprint cycles. I defined the segmentation logic (post founder discussion) and content requirements as part of the build team.

Impact
Activation rate (form start)55% → 68%
Organic-cohort uninstall rate↓ ~22%

V1 shipped in 2–3 months; iteration continued for 6+ months.

What I'd do differently
  • Female cohort: instrument cohort-level attribution earlier — we knew it worked before we could cleanly isolate which touchpoint drove the most lift.
  • Login module: ship the WhatsApp retention flow before the module — the segmentation was ready; we could have recovered lost M2 users ~6 weeks sooner with just the messaging layer.
  • App onboarding: A/B test video length earlier — we assumed more context was better; shorter may have won.

Every experiment followed the same pattern: a metric that looked like a product problem was really a user-understanding problem; the fix was a system change across touchpoints, not a single feature; and the impact compounded — female revenue, retention LTV, and organic activation each became a structural advantage rather than a one-time lift.
